# Visual and interaction craft

## Direction before decoration
- Identify brand personality, audience, value proposition and desired emotional response.
- Establish a deliberate visual hierarchy: headline/value, supporting proof, primary action, secondary paths.
- Prefer a recognizable art direction over interchangeable gradient-heavy AI layouts.
- Use authentic product imagery and context-rich demonstrations; avoid fake testimonials or invented social proof.

## Systematic execution
- Reuse brand typography, spacing, radii, semantic colors and component states from repository tokens.
- Use typography scale, comfortable measure, alignment and rhythm to create perceived quality.
- Create clear distinction among primary, secondary, tertiary and destructive actions.
- Use micro-interactions for cause/effect: selection, success, validation, progress and transitions.
- Respect `prefers-reduced-motion`; keep animation optional and non-blocking.
- Favor responsive composition, not just shrinking desktop; prioritize touch ergonomics and sticky actions judiciously.
- Prefer real content and realistic data lengths; check localization expansion.

## Distinctive concepts
For open-ended explorations, offer 2–3 approaches such as:
1. **Editorial premium:** strong photography, typography, whitespace, considered transitions.
2. **Guided confidence:** interactive comparison, progressive selection, educational microcopy.
3. **Expressive product demo:** purposeful animation, contextual interactive demonstration, restrained 3D.

Choose by customer job and technical budget; do not apply all three indiscriminately.

## Anti-patterns
Decorative scroll hijacking; unskippable intros; vague CTAs; invisible controls; excessive carousels; motion during form completion; layout shifts; sticky overlays hiding content; gratuitous modals; inaccessible custom selects; surprise costs; fabricated scarcity.
